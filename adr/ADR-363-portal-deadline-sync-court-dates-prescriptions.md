# ADR-363: Portal deadline sync for court dates and prescriptions — visibility rule, events and backfill (extends ADR-256)

**Status**: Accepted

**Context**:

[ADR-256](ADR-256-polymorphic-portal-deadline-view.md) chose one polymorphic `portal.portal_deadline_view` table (global `V1__baseline.sql:103-118`: PK `(source_entity, id)`, `source_entity IN ('FILING_SCHEDULE','COURT_DATE','PRESCRIPTION_TRACKER','CUSTOM_FIELD_DATE')`, `deadline_type IN ('FILING','COURT_DATE','PRESCRIPTION','CUSTOM_DATE')`, `status IN ('UPCOMING','DUE_SOON','OVERDUE','COMPLETED','CANCELLED')`, `description_sanitised varchar(140)`) and said that adding a source is "a handler that writes to the table with the new `source_entity`". That handler never came: `DeadlinePortalSyncService` wires only `FieldDateApproachingEvent` (`:98-116`), its Javadoc lists COURT_DATE and PRESCRIPTION_TRACKER as dormant, and `grep publishEvent` over `verticals/legal/courtcalendar/` is empty — `CourtCalendarService` and `PrescriptionTrackerService` write audit events only and do not inject `ApplicationEventPublisher` (`CourtCalendarService.java:60`). The canonical write seam exists and is unused: `upsertFromDeadline(DeadlineSource)` (`:228-246`) with `DeadlineSource(sourceEntity, deadlineType, id, customerId, matterId, label, dueDate, overrideStatus, descriptionRaw, sourceReference)` (`:256-266`), `deriveStatus` (`:274-283`), the sanitiser ([ADR-254](ADR-254-portal-description-sanitisation.md)) and `PortalDeadlineViewRepository.deleteBySourceEntityAndId` (`:115`). The portal already renders all four source types (`portal/lib/api/deadlines.ts:14-31`, `GET /portal/deadlines`, `deadlines/page.tsx`). Register §5b row 19 asked for the sync (and a mark-read control); founder decision 6 builds the sync and decision 7 drops mark-read. Red test: `portal/e2e/tests/portal-client-90day/day-45-75.spec.ts:163-168`.

Three things ADR-256 left open must be decided here: who sees a deadline (the portal reads by `customer_id`, `PortalContact.customer_id NOT NULL`, while a matter has both `projects.customer_id` and `customer_projects` links), what text crosses to the portal (court dates carry `judge_magistrate`, `outcome` and free-text `description`; trackers carry `cause_of_action_date` and `interruption_reason`), and how existing future rows reach the table.

**Options Considered**:

1. **Polling job: a per-tenant scheduled scan of `court_dates` and `prescription_trackers` that re-upserts every future row (the `CourtDateReminderJob:163` query shape).**
   - Pros:
     - No change to the firm-side services; doubles as the backfill; `deriveStatus` is re-evaluated on every pass so `UPCOMING` → `DUE_SOON` transitions happen without a firm-side edit.
   - Cons:
     - Cancellations and postponements propagate only on the next tick — a client could see a court date that was cancelled an hour ago; the scenario walk ("cancelled court dates disappear") would be timing-dependent, which Phase 88 showed is a test variable.
     - A full-table scan per tenant per tick in a schema-per-tenant product, forever, to replace an event that fires once.
2. **Domain events published by the two services in the firm transaction, consumed AFTER_COMMIT by `DeadlinePortalSyncService`, plus a one-time marker-gated backfill runner for existing future rows (CHOSEN).**
   - Pros:
     - The mechanism every other portal projection uses ([ADR-032](ADR-032-spring-application-events-for-portal.md), [ADR-253](ADR-253-portal-surfaces-as-read-model-extensions.md)): only committed firm-side changes reach the portal; a sync failure is logged and never rolls back the firm transaction.
     - The service that knows the state transition publishes the right event — postpone, cancel and outcome are explicit, so the portal row is deleted or completed in the same request.
     - The backfill is the `PortalContactDirectoryBackfillRunner` pattern (`@Order(60)`, `@SchedulerLock`, per-tenant marker, never fails startup) and runs once.
   - Cons:
     - Four new records in the sealed `event/DomainEvent.java` permits list and `ApplicationEventPublisher` injected into two services; `deriveStatus` stays sync-time only (ADR-253), so an `UPCOMING` row does not become `DUE_SOON` until the firm edits it — accepted, as for custom-field dates.
     - The existing marker table `portal.portal_contact_directory_backfill` (global V2) is keyed on `tenant_schema` alone with no backfill-name column, so this backfill needs its own global marker table.
3. **Database triggers / CDC on `court_dates` and `prescription_trackers` writing into the portal schema.**
   - Pros:
     - Cannot be bypassed by a code path that forgets to publish.
   - Cons:
     - Cross-schema writes from tenant triggers into `portal.*` are exactly what the read-model isolation principle ([ADR-031](ADR-031-separate-portal-read-model-schema.md)) forbids; a trigger per tenant schema is a migration per tenant and has no access to the sanitiser or the customer-resolution fallback.
     - Status semantics (HEARD → COMPLETED, postpone = new row) are service knowledge, not row knowledge.

**Decision**: Option 2. **Events** — four sealed `DomainEvent` records: `CourtDateScheduledEvent` (on create; on update when date, type or court changed; for the **new** row of a postpone; on outcome recorded, carrying `status=HEARD` — only for a SCHEDULED row; a POSTPONED original has no portal row and publishes nothing), `CourtDateCancelledEvent` (on cancel; for the **original** row of a postpone), `PrescriptionDeadlineSetEvent` (on create; on update), `PrescriptionDeadlineClearedEvent` (on interrupt). Published by `CourtCalendarService` and `PrescriptionTrackerService`, which gain `ApplicationEventPublisher`. **Listeners** in `DeadlinePortalSyncService`: Scheduled / Set → `upsertFromDeadline` (HEARD → `overrideStatus = COMPLETED`); Cancelled / Cleared → `deleteBySourceEntityAndId`. Row id = the firm-side entity id; `sourceEntity` `COURT_DATE` / `PRESCRIPTION_TRACKER`; `deadlineType` `COURT_DATE` / `PRESCRIPTION`. **Visibility rule** — every portal contact of the customer that owns the matter sees every court date and prescription on it; owner = the entity's denormalised `customer_id` (`projects.customer_id`, set at instantiation `ProjectTemplateService.java:533`), the fallback `customerProjectRepository.findFirstCustomerByProjectId` is defensive only (`customer_id` is NOT NULL on both entities); no row when neither resolves; co-clients on a multi-client matter do **not** see them (one row per deadline, PK `(source_entity, id)`). **Copy rule (sanitised)** — court date label = humanised `date_type` + court name ("Hearing — Johannesburg High Court"); description = `court_reference` only; never judge/magistrate, outcome or free-text description. Prescription label = "Prescription deadline"; description = humanised `prescription_type`; never cause-of-action detail or interruption reason. Both pass through the ADR-254 sanitiser and the 140-character column. **Backfill** — `PortalDeadlineBackfillRunner` (marker `portal_deadline_backfill` in the shared global `portal.portal_backfill_markers(backfill_name, tenant_schema, backfilled_at, row_count)` table, global V3): court dates `status = 'SCHEDULED' AND scheduled_date >= today(SA)` (never POSTPONED: the live path deletes the original's row at postpone and the new SCHEDULED row carries the date); trackers not INTERRUPTED/EXPIRED with `prescription_date >= today(SA)`; via `upsertFromDeadline` (widened to public or the runner placed in `customerbackend.service`). **No mark-read control** (decision 7). **`FILING_SCHEDULE` stays dormant** ([ADR-330](ADR-330-archive-tag-not-fork.md): accounting filing is archived).

**Rationale**:

1. **The owning customer is the only identity the portal has.** `PortalContact.customer_id` is NOT NULL and `findByCustomer` scopes every read; a deadline row has one `customer_id`. `CourtDate` and `PrescriptionTracker` already store the matter's `projects.customer_id` at creation, so the owner is a column read, not a join; `customer_id` is NOT NULL on both entities, so the `customer_projects` fallback is defensive only and mirrors what the custom-date sync does (`resolveCustomerId`, `:285+`). Fan-out to co-clients would need N rows per deadline under a PK that admits one, and it is a product question (which co-client may see a hearing?) that nobody has asked — recorded in §14.12, not designed.
2. **A court date is the one deadline a client must never be surprised by, and the one whose detail must never leak.** The label and `court_reference` are what a client is told anyway; the judge's name, the recorded outcome and the attorney's free-text notes are firm work product and, for prescriptions, the cause of action and interruption reason are privileged. The copy rule is enumerated so a reviewer can check it against the `DeadlineSource` constructor call, and [ADR-254](ADR-254-portal-description-sanitisation.md)'s sanitiser still runs over what remains.
3. **Events, because state transitions carry meaning the row does not.** A postpone is two rows firm-side (`postponeCourtDate`, `:247-275`: original → POSTPONED, new SCHEDULED row), so the portal must delete one and upsert the other; `recordOutcome` → HEARD maps to the table's `COMPLETED`; `interrupt` ends a prescription. Only the service knows this at the moment it happens; a poller or a trigger would reconstruct it.
4. **Sealed list, not the trust vertical's standalone records.** The TODO in `DeadlinePortalSyncService` (`:118-127`) and `FieldDateApproachingEvent` both assume the sealed `DomainEvent` shape (`eventType, entityType, entityId, projectId, actorMemberId, actorName, tenantId, orgId, occurredAt, details`); keeping the four records there means `NotificationEventHandler` and the audit correlation key ([ADR-349](ADR-349-activity-feed-correlation-key.md): `project_id` in details is a publisher obligation) apply without a second listener style. The trust vertical's standalone sealed records remain the precedent for events that must not enter the core bus.
5. **Backfill as a runner, because the rows already exist and the portal is global.** The read model lives in the global `portal` schema, which a global migration must not populate from tenant schemas (BASELINE.md A8); a runner walking `org_schema_mapping` with a per-tenant marker is the only idempotent path that also works on a fresh shard. The marker table named by the pattern is `portal.portal_contact_directory_backfill(tenant_schema PK, backfilled_at, row_count)`; it has no marker-name column, so it cannot hold a second backfill's marker. Phase 89 therefore adds one shared global table `portal.portal_backfill_markers(backfill_name varchar(64), tenant_schema varchar(63), backfilled_at timestamptz, row_count int, PRIMARY KEY (backfill_name, tenant_schema))` — **global V3 at build time (next free, never reserved)** — used by this runner (`portal_deadline_backfill`) and by the Phase 89 portal task backfill (`portal_task_backfill`, §14.3). The V2 directory marker table is left untouched (a working marker is not migrated). `today(SA)` is `LocalDate.now(clock)` ([ADR-358](ADR-358-product-wide-sa-business-clock.md)); the runner injects `Clock` like the sync service.
6. **Phase 88 lesson — integration tests per event on the real write path, and browser observation.** One test per event (create / update-date / postpone / cancel / outcome / tracker create / update / interrupt) creates rows through the service the UI uses and reads back `portal_deadline_view`; a backfill test seeds a future and a past court date, runs the runner twice and asserts one row and one marker. The walk observes Sipho's court date on `/deadlines` and its disappearance after cancellation.

**Consequences**:

- Positive:
  - §5b row 19 is built as amended; `day-45-75.spec.ts:166` is un-fixme'd and re-worded without mark-read (E6 docs PR).
  - ADR-256's "adding a source is a handler" claim becomes true for two of its three dormant sources; no change to the table, its CHECKs or the portal API (`PortalDeadlineResponse` unchanged).
  - `upsertFromDeadline` becomes the only write path for three sources; the sanitiser and status derivation are applied uniformly.
- Negative:
  - Four more records in the sealed permits list; two services gain a publisher dependency and their existing audit-only tests gain event assertions.
  - `deriveStatus` is sync-time only: an `UPCOMING` court date flips to `DUE_SOON`/`OVERDUE` only on a firm-side edit or a reminder-job re-publish — same limitation as custom-field dates, accepted; a re-derivation pass is a backlog item, not this phase.
  - A global marker table (V3) is required for a one-time runner; it is the shared `portal.portal_backfill_markers` table (decided — rulings §5), reused by the task backfill and by any future read-model backfill.
  - Co-clients see nothing; a multi-client matter's second client has no deadline view until a product ruling exists.
  - Prescription "status" is computed at read time firm-side (`computeEffectiveStatus`, `:279-292`); the portal row's status is derived from `prescription_date` at sync time and may lag the firm's WARNED/EXPIRED labels by design.
- Explicitly not done: no mark-read (decision 7), no `FILING_SCHEDULE` wiring, no structured court-venue column (ADR-256 Option 2 remains rejected), no portal notification e-mail for new deadlines ([ADR-258](ADR-258-portal-notification-no-double-send.md) and [ADR-350](ADR-350-client-emails-explicit-intent.md) unchanged), no widening of `description_sanitised`.
