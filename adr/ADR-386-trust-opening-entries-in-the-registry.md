# ADR-386: Trust opening entries are registry types cleared at cutover; items outstanding at cutover are matchable rows with offsets

**Status**: Accepted

**Context**:

Phase 92 made every trust balance a sum over posted rows whose effects are set once by the `TrustTransactionType` registry (ADR-378), and defined the equation per account as at a date: adjusted bank = cashbook (EQ-1) and cashbook = trust creditors, six terms (EQ-2), with EQ-3 to EQ-6 as supporting identities and gates G1–G4 on completion (ADR-377). There is no opening-balance concept anywhere. The requirements (requirements decision 2 (RD-2)) want every creditor group taken on — client and matter ledgers, the Fund interest-and-charges ledger, held unidentified deposits itemised, s86(3) and s86(4) investments — plus the bank items outstanding at cutover, and the acceptance invariant is that the first post-cutover reconciliation balances with no manual adjustment.

Three facts at HEAD shape the answer. (1) Outstanding items are posted rows that move the cashbook and are not matched to a statement line dated on or before the as-at date (`TrustLedgerQueryRepository.outstanding`, predicate at `:497`): an ordinary opening credit is therefore an outstanding deposit, and since the bank balance at cutover already contains the money, EQ-1 would be out by the bank balance. (2) The deferred Phase 92 "opening-balance snapshot" is a read-performance escape hatch (Phase 92 doc `:262`, `:2858`), not a cutover mechanism. (3) `chk_trust_txn_posting` requires every posted row to move one ledger by its amount and the cashbook by the same or nothing; amounts are positive (`chk_trust_txn_amount_positive`); every client posting needs a matter of that client (`TrustClientMatterRule`); and `PortalEmailNotificationChannel` emails the client on every recorded `DEPOSIT` (`:158`). The requirements also require opening types to be unreachable from normal trust entry points, and hard blocks with no override for a client in debit, an overall deficit, a control-total difference, an unidentified deposit without its date, and a client row without the client's matter.

**Options Considered**:

1. **Build the deferred snapshot: a per-account (and per-client, per-matter) opening table that every sum starts from.**
   - Pros: No rows at cutover; sums start from known figures.
   - Cons: A second source for every balance — position, client ledger, matter cell, investment balance, outstanding, statement of account, portal, Rule 54.15 list — each would need to become snapshot-aware; the snapshot was designed to bound query cost, not to carry creditor detail; outstanding items still need a home.
2. **Synthetic "cutover statement" lines that the opening rows are matched to.**
   - Pros: The outstanding query is untouched — the rows are simply "matched".
   - Cons: Fake bank lines enter the bank-statement domain, the match UI, the Section 35 pack and the statement-fit rules; a statement whose lines do not sum to its balances; the next statement's fit and opening logic would have to know to ignore it.
3. **A separate cutover-items table for outstanding items, unioned into the outstanding sum, with all opening rows cleared.**
   - Pros: No offset rows; items need no client attribution.
   - Cons: A second match target for bank lines (match, unmatch, auto-match, G4, every outstanding listing and report), so two code paths for one concept; a cancelled cutover cheque needs a new write-back flow.
4. **A "take-on clearing" creditor account holding unattributed outstanding items and their balancing row.**
   - Pros: Items need no client; matching machinery reused.
   - Cons: A third creditor account type and a non-zero-by-design-until-cleared ledger in EQ-2's `otherNonClient` term; the balancing row's sign varies, needing two types; a cancelled cheque leaves money on the clearing ledger that needs a second allocation step.
5. **Registry types with a `Clearance` attribute written to the row as `cleared_at_cutover`; each outstanding item a matchable row on its client's matter plus a cleared offset (chosen).**
   - Pros: Every balance stays a sum over rows, so every balance reader (pages, the Rule 54.15 list, integrity, portal, MCP) sees the opening position with no change; the outstanding query gains one predicate; outstanding items are ordinary rows, so auto-match, manual match, unmatch, reports and G4 work unchanged; a cancelled cheque is an ordinary reversal.
   - Cons: Eleven new constants (25 → 36); outstanding items must name their client and matter; a client's ledger shows two hidden rows per outstanding item; readers that list or sum *movements* (Receipts & Payments, Section 35 part 1, staff statements, the interest-and-charges schedule) would present the position as cutover-day receipts unless each folds it into one brought-forward figure.

**Decision**: Trust opening entries are eleven `TAKEON_*` registry constants with `Origin.TAKE_ON` and a new `Clearance` attribute (`BANK` or `AT_CUTOVER`) that `TrustPostingService.recordOpening` writes to `trust_transactions.cleared_at_cutover`; cleared rows are excluded from outstanding items and can never be matched; each item outstanding at cutover is posted as an outstanding, matchable row on its client's matter with a cleared offset of the opposite sign; all rows are dated at the cutover date; and commit is hard-blocked unless the batch ties to the old system's reconciled position to the cent with no client in debit and no deficit.

**Rationale**:

The constants, with their full attributes, are in architecture §17.2.8: `TAKEON_CLIENT`, `TAKEON_FUND`, `TAKEON_FUND_DEBIT`, `TAKEON_UNIDENTIFIED`, `TAKEON_INVESTMENT`, `TAKEON_FIRM_INV_OUT`, `TAKEON_FIRM_INV_IN` (all cleared, reversal `NONE`), and the pairs `TAKEON_PAY_UNPRES` (outstanding, reversal `IMMEDIATE` like `PAYMENT`) / `TAKEON_PAY_OFFSET` and `TAKEON_DEP_TRANSIT` (outstanding, reversal `NEEDS_APPROVAL` like `DEPOSIT`) / `TAKEON_DEP_OFFSET`. Clearance is stored on the row, like the effects, so no SQL needs to know which types are cleared — the ADR-378 principle. Option 5 is the only one that keeps Phase 92's single source of truth (rows) and its single definition of outstanding (unmatched rows), changing one predicate in one query; the worked example in architecture §17.3.13 shows the first month balancing, and that without the attribute EQ-1 would be out by exactly the bank balance. Approval is a new `AT_SIGN_OFF` value: there is no per-row maker-checker, because the batch's partner/director sign-off authorises the whole position (ADR-391), and every row is posted `APPROVED` with the signer as approver and the sign-off time as `approved_at`. `APPROVED` rather than `RECORDED` records the authorisation on each row and is what the investment flows demand of a placement (`TrustInvestmentService.java:593`, `:988`; ADR-387); `recordWithPair`'s exhaustive approval switch gains an `AT_SIGN_OFF -> null` arm. Only `TAKEON_CLIENT` is client-visible, so the portal shows the brought-forward balance the client knows and the item/offset pairs, which net to zero, stay hidden. Opening entries never reuse `DEPOSIT` and publish no `TrustTransactionRecordedEvent`, so no client email can fire.

Unreachability is structural, not a convention: `record` refuses `TAKE_ON`-origin types; `recordOpening` requires an `OpeningAuthority` whose constructor is package-private to the trust take-on package; an ArchUnit rule allows only `..takeon..` to call it; the type DTO carries `takeOnOnly` so pickers filter without a hard-coded list. After commit, `trust_accounts.take_on_cutover_date` makes `TrustPostingService` refuse any other posting dated on or before the cutover, so the signed position cannot be changed by back-dating. That makes the FU-14 question (postings before `opened_date`) irrelevant to take-on: no take-on row predates the cutover, and the unidentified deposit's original date lives on the item (ADR-387). The commit locks in Phase 92's global order (batch, client anchors in UUID order, accounts `FOR UPDATE`) and self-checks the posted position against the inputs before it commits.

The hard blocks are take-on-owned because at reconciliation a client in debit is only a warning (`CLIENT_LEDGER_IN_DEBIT`). Their basis is in the requirements' legal basis: no trust creditor may be in debit (Rule 54.14.9) and a debit must be reported immediately to the Council in writing with the reason and proof of rectification (Rule 54.14.11) — the block shows that duty; an overall deficit (Rules 54.14.8, 54.14.10); a control-total difference (the take-on would fail the firm's first Rule 54.10 balancing); an unidentified deposit without its date (the s87(4)(a) clock); a client balance without the client's matter (`TrustClientMatterRule`). A4's default — never override — is taken. A Fund ledger in debit is allowed and flagged, consistent with ADR-381 (founder decision FD-12).

**Consequences**:

- Positive:
  - The first post-cutover reconciliation balances against a statement opening at the cutover balance with no special case in reconciliation code.
  - Every balance — cashbook, ledgers, the Rule 54.15 list, the integrity check, EQ-1 to EQ-6, the portal balance — is right with no change.
  - Cutover items are matched by the existing auto- and manual match; a cancelled cheque or a bounced deposit is an ordinary reversal.
  - The database enforces one brought-forward balance per client and matter per account, and that cleared rows never touch a bank line.
- Negative:
  - The registry grows to 36 constants; registry and schema tests grow with it.
  - Firms must attribute each outstanding item to a client and matter (FD-18); investment movements in transit at cutover are refused.
  - Each outstanding item adds two rows the client does not see but the firm's ledger page shows.
  - Auto-match must include open take-on items beyond its ±7-day window (FD-3), touching the same code FU-16 changes.
  - Movement readers are **not** right unchanged (correcting an earlier draft's claim that every reader was): Receipts & Payments and Section 35 part 1 exclude take-on rows and print one "Balance brought forward — take-on" line when the range includes the cutover; staff statements fold the client's take-on rows into one line; the interest-and-charges schedule's `addFundGroup` gains a take-on arm (its `default` would otherwise mark the month "does not balance"); the balance trend starts at the cutover month; `LedgerTotals` gains a signed `broughtForward` total and the two exhaustive type switches gain arms (architecture §17.3.15).
