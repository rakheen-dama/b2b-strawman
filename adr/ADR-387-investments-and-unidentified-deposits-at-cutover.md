# ADR-387: Investments and unidentified deposits at cutover keep their statutory facts

**Status**: Accepted

**Context**:

Two creditor groups carry facts the law cares about that a plain balance would lose.

**Unidentified deposits.** Under s87(4)(a), money whose owner is unknown or unclaimed is paid to the Fund after the second annual closing following the **deposit date** (requirements' legal basis, high confidence), so the original date must survive take-on and the clock must not restart at cutover. Phase 92 itemises unidentified deposits (`unidentified_deposits`, ADR-376), and each item already has its own `received_date` separate from its posting row's date. But at HEAD `bank_statement_line_id` is `NOT NULL`, `UNIDENTIFIED_DEPOSIT` is `recordedOnlyFromStatementLine()` (`TrustTransactionType.java:502`), and EQ-4 requires every row on the unidentified ledger to name its item. A held deposit received months before cutover has no statement line in Kazi and never will. The requirements also reject a single lump suspense balance.

**Investments.** Phase 92 put investments on the books (ADR-379): s86(4) money sits on an `INVESTMENT` trust account as a client investment creditor; s86(3) money appears as inter-account balances that cancel firm-wide (EQ-3); EQ-5 ties each investment's rows to its record, counting `principal` once `deposit_transaction_id` is posted (`TrustLedgerQueryRepository.java:596-600`). `TrustInvestment` has no field for the s86(4) client instruction or the Rule 54.17.2 written confirmation, no Rule 54.17.3 endorsement flag, no accrued interest and no Fund-share status. The legal basis: s86(4) interest goes to the client with 5% to the Fund (s86(5)); written confirmation is required (54.17.2); the account must be endorsed as opened under s86(4) (54.17.3); for s86(3) interest goes to the Fund. Confidence is medium on making the reference mandatory; A5 (accrued interest and Fund-share status at cutover) is an open auditor question.

**Options Considered**:

1. **Unidentified deposits as one lump Fund-ledger or suspense balance; investments as plain client balances.**
   - Pros: Two fewer section formats; no schema change.
   - Cons: Loses the per-deposit date (the s87(4) clock) and breaks EQ-4; the requirements reject a lump balance; investments vanish from the investment register, EQ-5 and the Rule 54.15 comparison with investments.
2. **Fabricate a statement line for each unidentified deposit, and post investment placements through the normal placement flow at cutover.**
   - Pros: Reuses the statement-line and placement paths as they are.
   - Cons: Fake bank lines in the bank-statement domain; a placement at cutover posts `INV_PLACE` on the banking account (cashbook out) that the bank never shows — an outstanding payment that can never be matched; dates the investment at cutover instead of its real deposit date.
3. **Hold the original unidentified-deposit date only in the posting row's date (post at the original date).**
   - Pros: No new column semantics.
   - Cons: Postings before the cutover and before the account's `opened_date` (FU-14), and before the take-on boundary the account then enforces; the cutover position would no longer be "everything dated at C".
4. **Take-on items and investments as first-class records with their statutory fields, posted by dedicated cleared opening types at the cutover date (chosen).**
   - Pros: The s87(4) clock is the item's `received_date`, untouched; EQ-4 and EQ-5 hold by construction; the investment register, Rule 54.15 list and Rule 54.28 schedule see taken-on investments as they see any other; every posting is dated at the cutover.
   - Cons: `bank_statement_line_id` becomes nullable (guarded by a CHECK); four new investment columns; the firm must supply more per investment.

**Decision**: A held unidentified deposit is taken on as an `unidentified_deposits` item with no statement line, its original `received_date`, amount and bank reference or description, opened by a cleared `TAKEON_UNIDENTIFIED` row dated at the cutover that names it; an investment is taken on as a `trust_investments` record with its original deposit date, the client's principal, and — for s86(4) — a mandatory instruction or Rule 54.17.2 confirmation reference and date and a confirmed Rule 54.17.3 endorsement, with accrued uncapitalised interest recorded as a memo and Fund money already capitalised and not yet paid over posted as a Fund-ledger balance on the investment account. The investment's placement row (`TAKEON_INVESTMENT` for s86(4), `TAKEON_FIRM_INV_IN` for s86(3), its `deposit_transaction_id`) is posted **`APPROVED`, with the trust signer as approver** and the sign-off time as `approved_at`; the investment services' approved-placement checks are not relaxed.

**Rationale**:

The item, not the posting, carries the clock: Phase 92 already separated `received_date` from the row's `transaction_date`, and `UNIDENTIFIED_OVER_ONE_YEAR` reads `received_date`, so a taken-on item is flagged correctly from day one and the later allocate or pay-to-Fund flows (647D) work unchanged on a `HELD` item. `chk_unidentified_deposit_origin` — exactly one of a statement line or a take-on batch — keeps the "every item came from somewhere verifiable" property. A dedicated type is needed because `UNIDENTIFIED_DEPOSIT` is bank-originated and may only be created from a line; reusing it would weaken a Phase 92 invariant (652H) for every firm. Every take-on posting is dated at the cutover, so the FU-14 question does not arise.

For investments, the structure follows the Phase 92 model: s86(4) principal is a `TAKEON_INVESTMENT` row on the investment account (client investment creditor; the banking account has no row because the money left it before cutover), and s86(3) is a `TAKEON_FIRM_INV_IN` on the investment account and a `TAKEON_FIRM_INV_OUT` on the banking account, so EQ-3 nets to zero and the banking account's creditors still include the clients' full balances, as Rule 54.15.1 compares creditors with bank plus investments. `deposit_transaction_id` is the take-on row, so EQ-5 counts the principal from the cutover. Interest the bank has already capitalised splits by owner: the client's share is in `principal`; the Fund's share (the s86(5) 5% for s86(4), all of it for s86(3)) not yet paid over is `fund_amount_at_take_on`, posted as `TAKEON_FUND` on the investment account's Fund ledger — so the account's cashbook equals the institution's confirmation and the Fund's money is on the Fund's ledger, where a later sweep debits it. Accrued interest the bank has not capitalised is not trust money in the account yet; posting it would make the cashbook exceed the confirmation, so it is a memo on the investment and on the take-on report, and when the bank capitalises it the firm records it through the existing gross/net interest entry (which applies the 5%). That is the default for A5 (FD-13). The s86(4) instruction fields are mandatory at take-on (CHECK `chk_trust_invest_take_on_instruction`, FD-14) and optional for investments placed in Kazi, whose form is unchanged this phase. Investment movements in transit at cutover are refused: they cannot be represented as one account's cleared position.

**Approved placement.** `withdrawInvestment` and the interest entry both refuse unless the investment's `deposit_transaction_id` row is `APPROVED` and posted (`TrustInvestmentService.java:593`, `:988`) — a Phase 92 guard that an unapproved placement cannot earn interest or be withdrawn. A take-on row posted `RECORDED` would fail both checks, so a taken-on investment could never be credited with the bank's interest or withdrawn, its statement lines would stay unresolved, and G4 would block every later reconciliation of the investment account. Two ways out were weighed. *Relaxing the checks* (accept `RECORDED` when the row is `Origin.TAKE_ON`) puts a take-on branch into two money-moving guards and weakens a Phase 92 invariant for every investment to serve a one-off migration. *Posting the placement `APPROVED`* states a fact: the placement was authorised — by the s86(4) instruction the firm confirms at take-on, and in Kazi by the partner/director sign-off of the whole position (Rule 54.19), whose member and time are exactly what `approved_by`/`approved_at` record. `APPROVED` is already a posted status (`chk_trust_txn_posting`), so no constraint changes. Neither check looks for an `INV_PLACE` or at the funding account: both read the investment's `deposit_transaction_id` (`TrustInvestmentRepository.findPlacementTransactionIdById`, `:43-44`; `getDepositTransactionId()` at `:981`), and withdrawal takes the matter from that row and the amount from the investment's rows — so the take-on commit sets `deposit_transaction_id`, the row's `investment_id`, client and matter, and the investment's funding and investment accounts, and no lookup changes. The second is chosen, and is applied to every take-on row (approval `AT_SIGN_OFF`, ADR-386) so the rows are uniform. Architecture §17.3.13 works an interest entry, a reconciliation and a withdrawal on a taken-on s86(4) investment; `TakenOnInvestmentLifecycleIntegrationTest` (657B) runs it.

**Unidentified items after the cutover.** `deposit_transaction_id` of a taken-on item is its `TAKEON_UNIDENTIFIED` row, so the item's lock (`findByDepositTransactionIdForUpdate`) and unique index behave as for statement items. Its resolution date window is floored at `max(received_date, cutover + 1 day)` (`UnidentifiedDepositService.requireDateInWindow`, `:616-643`), because a resolution dated on or before the cutover would be refused by the cutover guard. `bank_description` stays `NOT NULL`, falling back to "Unidentified deposit brought forward — take-on (ref {bank reference})" when the file has no description.

**Shared investment account.** An investment account funded from two banking accounts is taken on by both batches at the same cutover date and before its first reconciliation; each batch posts the investments it lists, keyed by the investment (institution and account number) so neither can post one twice.

**Consequences**:

- Positive:
  - The s87(4) clock and the s86(4) evidence survive take-on; the auditor sees both on the take-on report.
  - EQ-4 and EQ-5 hold after take-on with no special case.
  - Taken-on investments behave like Kazi-placed ones afterwards (interest entries, withdrawal, register, schedule), because their placement row is `APPROVED` like any approved Kazi placement.
  - Every take-on row names the signer as its approver, so the row itself shows who authorised it.
- Negative:
  - The unidentified-deposit line FK is nullable; code that assumed a line must now handle a take-on item (the allocate dialog's "bank line" link, the Section 35 pack's evidence column).
  - Firms must supply per-investment detail, including a confirmation balance, and settle in-transit movements first.
  - Accrued interest is a memo the firm must later record from the bank's advice; nothing reminds it beyond the report.
  - A5 and FD-14 may be answered differently by an auditor; either change is a copy/CHECK change, not a model change.
  - `approved_by` on a take-on row means "authorised by the take-on sign-off", not a per-row review; the take-on report and the audit trail (`takeon.batch.signed_off`) say so.
  - Two batches sharing an investment account must both commit before that account is first reconciled.
