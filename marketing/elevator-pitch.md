# Kazi — Elevator Pitch

> One narrative, told at three lengths. Order is fixed (per the GTM plan): **pain → AI moment → take-on close → continuity if asked**. Never lead with feature breadth — 40 modules reads as risk to a small firm, not value.

## The 10-second version

> "Kazi runs a small law firm's back office — matters, fee notes, trust accounting, FICA — and it's the first system in South Africa where you can point your own AI at your practice. Switching is our job: we move your data for you."

## The 30-second version

> "You know the three worst days of your month: the fee-note run, the trust reconciliation, and chasing the debtors who ignored the last statement. Kazi is practice management built for small South African firms that makes those days ordinary — time and matters flow straight into fee notes, the trust ledger stays reconciled and auditor-ready, FICA is a checklist instead of a filing cabinet.
>
> And here's the part nobody else here has: you can point your own AI at your practice. Ask Claude to draft the month's fee notes, screen the trust account against s86, or triage a new matter for conflicts — grounded in your real data, with a human approving everything.
>
> Switching is our job, not yours — we move your clients, matters, and balances for you. We're taking on two or three design-partner firms this quarter, free for six months."

## The 2-minute version

Use the 30-second version, then extend:

**On trust accounting (the credibility beat):**
> "Trust is where we spent the engineering. Every payment out of trust is approval-gated, every entry is on an immutable audit trail, and the system holds the three-way reconciliation — bank, cashbook, client ledgers — continuously, not once a month in Excel. When your auditor arrives, the file is already built."

**On the AI layer (the demo hook):**
> "The AI isn't a chatbot bolted onto a dashboard. Your firm's Kazi account exposes a secure, read-only connection that your own Claude can use. There are ready-made skills: file an email into the right matter, draft fee notes from unbilled time in your house style with VAT and contingency caps applied, screen the trust ledger for anomalies, check a client's FICA gaps and draft the request letter. Anything that writes back requires a human to approve it inside Kazi. I'd rather show you than tell you — it takes five minutes."

**On take-on (the close):**
> "The reason firms stay on software they hate is the move. So the move is our deliverable: you give us your client list, open matters, debtor balances, and trust balances — from GhostPractice, AJS, Lexpro, or your bookkeeper's spreadsheet — and I personally do the take-on. The trust take-on is auditor-defensible: control totals must tie to your bank balance at cutover before anything commits, and you get an exportable take-on report."

**On continuity (only when the objection surfaces — and it will):**
> "Fair question: small vendor, trust data. Three honest answers. One: your data is exportable in full, any time, by you — that's built, not promised. Two: there's a documented take-off path — if you leave, you leave with everything, in usable formats. Three: the pilot agreement puts that exit clause in writing. I'd rather you sign knowing you can leave than hesitate because you can't."

**The ask:**
> "We're signing two or three design-partner firms this quarter. Free for six months, I do the onboarding personally, and in return I want thirty minutes of feedback a week and permission to name you as a reference when we launch. After the pilot, pricing is a flat firm-level fee — around R2,500 to R5,000 a month depending on size, unlimited users, no per-seat games."

## Audience variants

**To a trust-account auditor** (channel #3 — they refer):
> "You see the same mess in every small firm you audit: spreadsheet reconciliations, missing FICA files, fee notes done at midnight. Kazi keeps the trust account continuously reconciled three ways with an immutable audit trail — the audit file is a download, not a scramble. I'd value your scrutiny before we scale it: if it survives your review, your clients' migrations get easier and your audits get shorter."

**To an advisor / introducer** (channel #2):
> "I'm looking for two or three small firms willing to be design partners — free for six months, I do the data migration personally. What I need from you is one introduction to a managing partner who's sick of month-end. If it turns into a paying firm, we'll agree a referral arrangement."

## Objection one-liners

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already have GhostPractice / AJS / Lexpro." | "Keep it running — the pilot is parallel and free. If Kazi doesn't make month-end obviously better within a quarter, you've lost nothing and we part with a full export." |
| "What if you get hit by a bus?" | "Full data export any time, documented take-off path, exit clause in the pilot letter. You can always leave — that's deliberate." |
| "AI and client confidentiality / POPIA?" | "The AI connection is opt-in per firm, consent-gated, read-only by default, and every write-back needs human approval inside Kazi. Nothing trains on your data." |
| "We're too small for software like this." | "Flat firm fee, unlimited users. It's priced for a five-person firm, not a listed one." |
| "Our bookkeeper runs everything through Excel." | "Then your bookkeeper is who I onboard first. The take-on wizard eats spreadsheets — that's the point." |

## Do not say

- Feature counts, module counts, phase counts. (Reads as risk.)
- "Disrupt", "revolutionize", or any claim about incumbents' demise.
- Customer counts or traction we don't have. The honest frame is: "new, built deep, looking for design partners who shape it."
- Per-seat pricing of competitors as an attack line — let the flat fee speak.
