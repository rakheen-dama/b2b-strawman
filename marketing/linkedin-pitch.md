## Post 1 — The pain post (launch)

> Every small law firm I've spoken to has the same three worst days of the month.
>
> The fee-note run: an evening (or three) reconstructing what everyone actually did from timesheets, sticky notes, and memory.
>
> The trust reconciliation: an Excel file, a bank statement, and a quiet prayer that the three-way tie works out — because if it doesn't, that's not an admin problem, that's a Legal Practice Act problem.
>
> The debtors: last month's fee notes, unpaid, and nobody whose job it is to chase them.
>
> I've spent 18 months building Kazi to make those days ordinary. Time flows into fee notes. The trust ledger stays reconciled three ways, continuously, on an immutable audit trail. Debtors get statements and follow-ups on a schedule instead of when someone remembers.
>
> I'm looking for 2–3 small SA firms to be design partners — free for six months, and I do the data migration myself. If month-end at your firm looks like the above, my DMs are open.

## Post 3 — The switching-cost post

> Why do firms stay on practice software they complain about for a decade?
>
> It's not the monthly fee. It's the move. Ten years of clients, open matters, debtor balances — and a trust ledger where a migration mistake isn't an inconvenience, it's a compliance finding.
>
> So with Kazi I made the move the product. You hand over exports (GhostPractice, AJS, Lexpro, or your bookkeeper's spreadsheets — CSV is fine). I run the take-on personally. The trust take-on refuses to commit until the client balances tie to your actual bank balance at cutover, to the cent, and you get an auditor-ready take-on report at the end.
>
> And the same door swings both ways: full data export, any time, built into the product. You should stay because it's good, not because you're stuck.
>
> Looking for 2–3 design-partner firms this quarter. Free pilot, migration included.

## Post 4 — The honest continuity post

> The hardest question a managing partner can ask me: "You're one person. What happens to our trust data if you get hit by a bus?"
>
> Here's my honest answer, and it's in the pilot agreement:
>
> 1. Your data is exportable in full, by you, any time — clients, matters, documents, ledgers. Built, not promised.
> 2. There's a documented take-off path: if you leave, you leave with everything in usable formats.
> 3. The pilot has an explicit exit clause. No lock-in, no hostage negotiation.

> I'd rather a firm sign knowing exactly how they'd leave than hesitate because they can't. That's also, frankly, the standard I wish more software was held to.
